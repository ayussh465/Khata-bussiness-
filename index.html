E-Way Bill Validity Calculator
Rule 138 — validity period from distance

Inputs

Normal cargo1 day / 200 km
Over-dimensional1 day / 20 km
Under Rule 138 of the CGST Rules: for normal cargo, validity is 1 day for every 200 km (or part thereof); for over-dimensional cargo, 1 day for every 20 km (or part thereof). Part of a day counts as a full day. The relevant date/time is when the e-way bill is generated, and validity can be extended by the transporter within the permitted window before/after expiry.

Depreciation — Block of Assets
Sec 32, Income Tax Act — WDV method

Block details

5%
10%
15%
40%
Under Sec 32, depreciation is computed on the WDV of the block of assets, not per-asset. Assets put to use for 180 days or more in the year get the full rate; assets used for less than 180 days get half the rate. If the block value goes to zero or negative (asset sold for more than WDV + additions), the balance is a short-term capital gain and no depreciation is allowed that year.

Partner's Remuneration
Sec 40(b) — maximum allowable remuneration

Inputs

Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972

Inputs

For employees covered under the Act: Gratuity = (15 × last drawn salary × completed years of service) / 26, with service of 6+ months rounded up to the next full year, subject to a statutory exemption cap of ₹20,00,000. Presumptive Taxation
Sec 44AD (business) & Sec 44ADA (profession)

Inputs

44ADBusiness
44ADAProfession

Compare with actual profit (optional)

Sec 44AD — presumptive income is 6% of turnover received digitally/by banking channels and 8% of turnover received in cash, provided total turnover is within ₹3 crore (₹2 crore if using the old limit) — no need to maintain books or get a tax audit if you declare at least these rates. Sec 44ADA — for specified professionals, presumptive income is 50% of gross receipts, provided receipts are within ₹75 lakh (₹50 lakh under the old limit) — same audit/books exemption applies. These are simplified checks; final eligibility depends on turnover thresholds, cash-receipt proportion, and other conditions under the Act.

HUF Tax Calculator
Hindu Undivided Family — Old vs New Regime

Income details

Use the Section 80 Deductions Calculator below to work out the Chapter VI-A figure above if you haven't already.
Key HUF-specific point: an HUF is taxed on the same slabs as an individual under both regimes, but it is not eligible for the Section 87A rebate — that rebate applies only to resident individuals. So even where an individual's tax would be reduced to Nil (taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 under the new regime), an HUF with the same taxable income still pays tax at slab rates. HUFs also don't get any age-based higher exemption, since an HUF has no "age".
Old Regime slabs used: 0–2.5L Nil · 2.5–5L 5% · 5–10L 20% · above 10L 30%. New Regime slabs (FY 2025-26): 0–4L Nil · 4–8L 5% · 8–12L 10% · 12–16L 15% · 16–20L 20% · 20–24L 25% · above 24L 30%. Both add 4% Health & Education Cess. Chapter VI-A deductions (80C, 80D, etc.) apply only under the Old Regime; the New Regime taxable income here is taken as Gross Total Income as-is (HUFs typically have no salary/pension income, so no standard deduction is assumed — adjust manually if that applies to your case).

Depreciation Schedule — Schedule II
Companies Act, 2013 — full year-wise schedule generator

Asset details

SLMStraight-line
WDVWritten-down value
Under Schedule II to the Companies Act, 2013, depreciation is computed over the useful life prescribed in Part C (or a technically justified different life, with disclosure), with the residual value not exceeding 5% of original cost. In the year of purchase/sale, depreciation is pro-rated for the number of days the asset was actually in use during that financial year (1 Apr–31 Mar). This schedule is illustrative — always align the useful life and method with the company's board-approved depreciation policy.
